#what is diff asset and liabilities ?
An **asset** is something you own that has economic value, while a **liability** is something you owe to others.

| 				| Asset 							| Liability |
|---|---|---	|
| **Meaning** 	| What a person or business owns 	| What a person or business owes |
| **Effect** 	| Brings in or stores value (money in your favour) | Takes value out (money you must pay) |
| **Examples** 	| Cash, bank balance, property, stocks, mutual funds, inventory, equipment | Loans, mortgages, credit card dues, unpaid bills, salaries payable |
| **Balance sheet** | Left side 					| Right side |

**Simple example:** 
If you buy a 
	house for ₹50 lakh 
	with ₹20 lakh of your own money and a 
	₹30 lakh home loan, 
	the house is an asset (₹50 lakh) and 
	the loan is a liability (₹30 lakh).

**Net worth = Assets − Liabilities**, so in this case your net worth is ₹20 lakh.

The basic accounting equation ties it together:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

A common rule of thumb: an asset puts money in your pocket (rent, dividends, interest), while a liability takes money out (EMIs, interest payments). Something can be both, like a car that has resale value (asset) but costs you fuel, insurance and maintenance, or one bought with a loan.
